The Northern Star

A trilogy about Mindlink, a direct connection from brain to network, and the corporation that holds it. Abdul Haq is Part I of The End, published on its own.

Book One

The Beginning

The oil is gone. Civilization lives inside a network. One soldier is about to give up his body to defend it.

In 2058, Mindlink lets humanity live and work in cyberspace, and the corporation that built it holds civilization by the mind. John Raimey agrees to become the first Tank Major, a bionic soldier sent after a hacker who can kill without leaving the network.

For fans of Daniel Suarez’s Daemon and Richard K. Morgan’s Altered Carbon.

Available on Amazon: The Northern Star: The Beginning (opens in a new tab)

Book Two

Civil War

He gave up his body for his family. A decade later, the only thing left to save is his daughter.

Ten years on, the world Mindlink built is cracking, and Raimey — the first Tank Major — is coming apart with it.

For fans of Richard K. Morgan and Daniel Suarez.

Available on Amazon: The Northern Star: Civil War (opens in a new tab)

Book Three

The End

One last mission. Kill the man who made himself a god — and set his daughter free.

Thirty-five years after he became the first bionic soldier, Raimey marches on the man who bound civilization’s mind to his will.

For fans of Richard K. Morgan, Daniel Suarez, and Joe Haldeman’s The Forever War.

About

Mike Gullickson writes near-future science fiction about technology, power, and the people caught in between.

His Northern Star trilogy imagines a world where the internet has been replaced by a direct brain-to-network connection, and one corporation controls the mind of civilization. The Midwest Book Review called it "military sci-fi and futuristic cyber-reality at its best," and The New Podler Review of Books called the series "a science fiction masterpiece." The trilogy was later optioned. Abdul Haq, the opening story of The End, is also published on its own with illustrations by Eric Tilton.

He's also the author of The Seethe, a post-apocalyptic thriller about the night black hail fell from the sky, which the Portland Book Review called "unique and captivating... gritty and horrifying." Its companion novella is The Seethe: Kirksville.

Mike lives in Manhattan Beach, California, with his wife and kids.
